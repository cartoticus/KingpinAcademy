Pin numbers

Pins are numbered front to back, left to right, as you face them.

Pin names

PinNameWhy it matters
1HeadpinThe front pin. You usually need it for a strike.
5KingpinThe middle pin. Hard to reach, so hitting it is a sign of a good strike.
7, 10Corner pinsThe back corners. The hardest single-pin spares.
8, 9SleepersHidden directly behind the 2 or 3 pin. Easy to miss seeing.
1 + 3Pocket (right-handed)Where a right-hander aims for a strike: between the 1 and 3 pins.
1 + 2Pocket (left-handed)Where a left-hander aims for a strike. For a right-hander, hitting here is a "Brooklyn."

The game in one minute

A game has 10 frames. In each frame you get up to two balls to knock down all 10 pins. The highest possible score is 300.

TermWhat it meansHow it scores
StrikeAll 10 pins down with your first ball10 + your next two balls
SpareAll 10 pins down using both balls10 + your next ball
Open framePins still standing after two ballsJust the pins you knocked down
SplitAfter the first ball, the headpin is down and the pins left standing have a gap between themScored like any other frame
MarkA strike or a spare—
10th frameStrike or spare here and you get bonus balls (up to three balls total)Bonus balls count only in the 10th

Lane etiquette

  • Yield to the right. If the bowler on the lane next to you is ready at the same time, the one on the right goes first.
  • One bowler on the approach. Wait in the seating area until it's your turn.
  • Never cross the foul line. The lane is oiled and slippery past it.
  • Be ready when it's your turn, but wait until the pinsetter has finished.
  • Use your own ball. Don't pick up someone else's ball without asking.
  • No food or drinks on the approach, and keep your shoes dry.
  • Roll the ball, don't drop or throw it. Dropping it on the lane damages the boards.
  • Stay on your lane and don't distract bowlers on other lanes.
  • Cheer for everyone, including the other team.

Safety and gear

  • Wear bowling shoes. Street shoes damage the approach and can make you slip.
  • Pick up the ball with two hands from the ball return, with your palms on the sides.
  • Keep fingers clear of the ball return so they don't get pinched between balls.
  • Choose the right weight: about 10% of your body weight, up to 16 lb. If you can't hold it out in front of you for a few seconds, go lighter.
  • Check the fit: your thumb should slide out easily, and your fingers shouldn't stretch to reach the holes.
  • Never go down the lane to fix a pin or get a ball. Ask the front desk.

Practice on your own

Practice

Practice with a plan. Pick one thing to work on, run the session below, and finish with a written note about what you felt. Bring your questions to your coach.

60-minute solo practice session

TimeWhat to do
0–5Stretch, then 5 shadow swings with no ball
5–15Foul-Line Drill — 10 shots, hold every finish
15–30Your one focus for today, using the drill that matches it
30–45Spares only — 3-6-9 Circuit or Corner-Pin Ladder
45–55Play one game with your full pre-shot routine
55–60Write one thing that worked and one thing to try next time

One focus per session. If you try to fix three things at once, none of them stick.

Rule of 31

Take the oil pattern length in feet and subtract 31. The answer is the board where the ball should exit the pattern at the breakpoint.

Pattern length − 31 = exit board

A 40-ft house shot: 40 − 31 = board 9 breakpoint. A 35-ft short sport: exit at board 4 — play outside. A 45-ft long pattern: board 14 — keep the ball in front of you and play straighter through the middle.

It's a starting point, not a law — surface, speed, and rev rate move it. Verify against real sheets in the Kegel Pattern Library.

Glossary

Breakpoint
The furthest-outside board the ball reaches before hooking back to the pocket.
Brooklyn
A strike hit on the wrong side of the headpin (1–2 pocket for a righty).
Double / Turkey / Hambone
Two, three, and four strikes in a row.
Entry angle
The angle the ball enters the pocket; ~4–6° maximizes strike percentage.
Laydown point
The board where the ball first touches the lane — roughly 6 boards right of your slide foot for a righty.
Loft
Distance the ball travels in the air past the foul line before touching down.
Pin deck
The area at the end of the lane where the pins stand.
Rev rate
Revolutions per minute the bowler imparts; more revs = more hook potential.
Sleeper
A pin hidden directly behind another (e.g., the 8 behind the 2).
Sport pattern
A flat, low-ratio oil pattern used in competition; demands true accuracy.
Transition
How the oil pattern changes as games are bowled — carrydown and burn.
Washout
A headpin-included split-like leave, e.g., 1-2-4-10.
