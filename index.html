Physics sandbox

Lane Simulator

Watch how oil pattern, laydown board, ball speed, and rev rate change ball motion. The pocket for a right-hander sits at board 17.5 (the 1–3 pocket). Your laydown point sits roughly 6 boards right of your slide foot — the default here is board 11, a classic house-shot line over 2nd arrow.

Set your line and throw.

Coach's tip

On a house shot, oil in the middle and dry outside gives you miss room: pull it inside and the oil holds the line, leak it right and the dry boards hook it back. Sport patterns take that forgiveness away — accuracy has to be real.

3-6-9 spare system

Spare Lab

Tap pins to knock them down and leave yourself a spare. The lab names the key pin and tells you the 3-6-9 move: keep your strike target at the 2nd arrow, move only your feet. Right-handed setup.

Ten-frame scoring engine

Score School

Enter a game ball by ball with the pinpad. Strikes score 10 plus your next two balls; spares score 10 plus your next one. Watch the cumulative math fill in only when it's actually earned — just like a real scorer's table.

Practice on your own

Practice

Practice with a plan. Pick one thing to work on, run the session below, and finish with a written note about what you felt. Bring your questions to your coach.

Rule of 31

Take the oil pattern length in feet and subtract 31. The answer is the board where the ball should exit the pattern at the breakpoint.

Pattern length − 31 = exit board

A 40-ft house shot: 40 − 31 = board 9 breakpoint. A 35-ft short sport: exit at board 4 — play outside. A 45-ft long pattern: board 14 — keep the ball in front of you and play straighter through the middle.

It's a starting point, not a law — surface, speed, and rev rate move it. Verify against real sheets in the Kegel Pattern Library.

Glossary

Breakpoint
The furthest-outside board the ball reaches before hooking back to the pocket.
Brooklyn
A strike hit on the wrong side of the headpin (1–2 pocket for a righty).
Double / Turkey / Hambone
Two, three, and four strikes in a row.
Entry angle
The angle the ball enters the pocket; ~4–6° maximizes strike percentage.
Laydown point
The board where the ball first touches the lane — roughly 6 boards right of your slide foot for a righty.
Loft
Distance the ball travels in the air past the foul line before touching down.
Pin deck
The area at the end of the lane where the pins stand.
Rev rate
Revolutions per minute the bowler imparts; more revs = more hook potential.
Sleeper
A pin hidden directly behind another (e.g., the 8 behind the 2).
Sport pattern
A flat, low-ratio oil pattern used in competition; demands true accuracy.
Transition
How the oil pattern changes as games are bowled — carrydown and burn.
Washout
A headpin-included split-like leave, e.g., 1-2-4-10.
